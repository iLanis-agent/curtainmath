CurtainMath

Honest curtain math: how many panels, how long, and why one panel per window never looks like the photo.

The problem

Curtains are the most-returned decor item for a reason: the product photo shows two windows' worth of fabric on one window, the length assumes a rod height nobody measured, and "fits 48-inch windows" describes a flat sheet, not a drape. The real math is fullness - fabric width against window width - and stores make you do it in your head.

What it does

Enter the window width and where the rod will actually hang. Pick a look - flat, classic, or lush - and CurtainMath returns the panel count (always in symmetric pairs), the hem length for float, kiss, break, or puddle styles, and the rod size including the foot of stack-back each side that keeps open panels off the glass.

The honest rules

Fullness is 1.5x, 2x, or 2.5x - there is no cheaper way to get the look. Panels round up to pairs. Rods get 12 inches of stack-back per side and then round up to the next stock size, because a rod at its max span sags. Puddling is six inches of floor-dwelling fabric and you should hear that before buying it.
